The key word is *draft*. You still add your voice, your story, your neighbourhood references. AI gives you raw material in seconds; you spend two minutes making it sound like you. That's the realistic day-to-day picture — not magic, but genuinely useful.

Those unanswered reviews were quietly hurting her local search ranking the whole time.

## The Free AI Tools Worth Your Time (And What They're Actually Good For)

---

### ChatGPT Free — Best for Review Replies and Caption Drafts

**Cost:** Free. The free plan gives you access to GPT-4o mini, with limited access to GPT-4o depending on traffic.

**Best for:** Writing Google review responses, describing menu specials, drafting social captions, and answering "what should I post this week?" when your brain is empty.

**How to use it in your shop:** Open ChatGPT and type: *"Write me a warm, friendly Google review response for a coffee shop. The customer said: [paste review here]. Mention their specific comment and invite them back."* Repeat for every outstanding review. You can batch 15 responses in under an hour.

**Honest limitation:** The free plan has usage caps, and if you hit them mid-session you'll need to wait or switch tools. Do your AI writing in one focused sitting rather than dipping in and out throughout the day — that way you get the most out of your free allowance before it resets.

---

### Google Gemini Free — Best for Content Planning and Menu Copy

**Cost:** Free at gemini.google.com, no account upgrade required for basic use.

**Best for:** Planning your content for the week, writing email newsletters to your regulars, and generating seasonal menu descriptions. Particularly useful if you already use Gmail or Google Docs, since it integrates directly.

**How to use it in your shop:** Ask it: *"Write me five Instagram captions for a coffee shop promoting our new winter drinks menu. Friendly tone, local feel, no hashtag spam."* You'll get five options in about 15 seconds. Pick the closest to your voice, edit two lines, and it's ready to post.

**Honest limitation:** Gemini's free version occasionally produces responses that feel slightly corporate or generic — less personality than you'd expect. You'll need to nudge it toward warmth in your prompt, or just edit the tone yourself. Once you've done it a few times, it takes about 30 seconds and becomes second nature.

---

### Canva Free — Best for Social Posts and Seasonal Menu Boards

**Cost:** Free. The paid Pro tier exists, but the free version is genuinely capable for most coffee shop needs.

**Best for:** Creating Instagram posts, seasonal menu boards, loyalty card designs, and promotional flyers — all without a designer or any design experience.

**How to use it in your shop:** Search "coffee shop Instagram post" in Canva's template library. Choose a template, swap in your photo and today's special, change the font colour to match your branding, and you're done in under five minutes. Canva's free AI text generator — built into the editor — can also write your caption while you're there, so you're designing and copywriting in one place.

**Honest limitation:** Some of the most polished, professionally designed templates are locked behind the paid Pro plan (around £10–£13/month depending on your region). The free library is still large enough to produce good-looking posts, but you'll occasionally spot a template you want and hit a paywall. Work around this by saving two or three free templates you like and using them consistently — consistency in your visual style matters more than variety anyway.

---

### Notion AI Free Tier — Best for Content Calendars and Menu Idea Storage

**Cost:** Free plan available. AI features are included with a limited number of AI responses per month on the free tier.

**Best for:** Building a simple content calendar, saving your best-performing post templates, and keeping seasonal menu ideas organised in one place rather than scattered across notes apps and sticky notes.

**How to use it in your shop:** Create a page called "Monthly Content Plan." Ask Gemini or ChatGPT to generate 20 post ideas for the coming month based on your upcoming promotions and seasonal drinks. Paste the best ones into Notion, assign them to dates, and you have a visible plan that stops the Sunday evening panic.

**Honest limitation:** Free AI responses are capped each month, and the cap isn't generous. For a coffee shop owner posting three times a week and using Notion purely for planning, the free allowance is usually enough. If you start using it for everything — drafting, planning, note-taking — you'll hit the ceiling faster than expected and either need to upgrade or use a different tool for the drafting work.

---

### Yelp and Google Business Profile Built-In AI Reply Suggestions — Best for Review Management on Autopilot

**Cost:** Completely free. Both are built into dashboards you should already be using.

**Best for:** Keeping on top of Google and Yelp reviews without it becoming a second job or a source of guilt every time you log in.

**How to use it in your shop:** Google Business Profile now includes AI-assisted reply suggestions directly inside the dashboard. When you log in to respond to a review, a draft reply is already waiting. Edit it, add one personalised detail — the drink they mentioned, the day they came in, something specific — and post it. The whole process takes under 60 seconds per review when you're in a rhythm.

**Honest limitation:** The AI-generated suggestions are generic by default. If you post them without editing, your regulars will notice — and it reads as cold rather than engaged. The fix is simple: always add one real, specific detail from the review before posting. That one edit is the difference between a reply that builds loyalty and one that feels automated.

---

### Copy.ai Free Plan — Best for Email Newsletters and Promotional Offers

**Cost:** Free plan available. Gives you a limited number of "runs" (generations) per month — enough for weekly use if you're focused.

**Best for:** Writing short promotional emails to your regulars, drafting loyalty reward announcements, and creating seasonal offer copy faster than starting from scratch.

**How to use it in your shop:** Select the "Email" template inside Copy.ai, fill in your product (e.g. "25% off all hot drinks on Tuesdays in January"), your tone ("friendly, local, warm"), and your target audience ("regular coffee shop customers"). It generates three or four variations in seconds. Pick the best one, personalise the opening line with your shop's name, and it's ready to send.

**Honest limitation:** Copy.ai's free plan limits you to a set number of monthly generations, and those limits are tighter than they used to be as the platform has pushed users toward paid plans. If you're only using it for one or two emails a month, the free tier holds up. For anything more frequent, you'll either hit the cap or find yourself needing to switch to ChatGPT or Gemini for the overflow.

## How to Get Started With Free AI Tools in Your Coffee Shop (No Tech Knowledge Needed)

1. **Start with your Google review backlog.** Log into your Google Business Profile, find your unanswered reviews, and open ChatGPT in another tab. Paste each review in, ask for a response, edit it to sound like you, and post it. Clearing a backlog of 15–20 reviews takes under an hour and immediately signals to Google that your business is active — which matters for local search.

2. **Set up free accounts for Canva and Gemini.** You need an email address and nothing else. Don't try to learn everything on day one — pick one task each (Canva for visuals, Gemini for captions) and use them for that single purpose for the first two weeks.

3. **Ask Gemini for a month of post ideas.** Prompt it with: *"Give me 20 coffee shop social post ideas for [month] focused on community, seasonal drinks, and behind-the-scenes content."* Paste the best ones into a Notion page or a simple Google Doc. That's your content calendar.

4. **Batch your content once a week.** Set aside one 45-minute slot — Sunday evening or Monday morning before you open. Use AI to draft captions for the week, edit them to sound like you, and either schedule them through your social platform or save them to post day by day. Batching beats daily scrambling every time.

5. **Make Friday your review day.** Five minutes, every Friday, to respond to any new reviews using Google's built-in suggestions plus one personal edit. Google rewards active profiles with better local search visibility. That consistency is free SEO with a measurable payoff.
