# How Accountants and Bookkeepers Use AI to Save Time on the Work Clients Never See (But Always Feel)

Your client doesn't care how long their management accounts took to produce. They care that they're accurate, on time, and actually useful — and right now, getting to that point is eating your week alive.

---

## The Real Time Problem in Accounting Isn't the Headline Work

Most accountants and bookkeepers assume their biggest time drain is tax season. In reality, it's everything around the core work: chasing missing receipts, writing the same client email for the fourth time this month, reformatting data exports, summarising lengthy reports into plain English, and explaining the same VAT question to a new client who swears they've never heard it before.

These tasks don't show up in your billing hours. They don't feel like "real" work. But stack them up across a week and you're easily looking at eight to twelve hours gone — before you've opened a single set of accounts.

---

## What AI Actually Does in an Accounting Practice

AI doesn't replace your judgement. What it does is eliminate the repetitive, low-skill work that surrounds every client file — drafting, summarising, formatting, translating numbers into narrative, and handling the predictable back-and-forth that clogs your inbox.

Think of a bookkeeper working with twenty small business clients. Every month, she exports figures from Xero, pastes them into a Word document, writes a short narrative explaining what's changed and why, and sends it off. That's forty minutes per client, every single month. With AI helping draft those narratives from the raw numbers, that process drops to under ten minutes. That's over ten hours a month handed back — from one workflow change.

The other place AI earns its keep is in client communication. Explaining tax codes, reconciliation queries, or reporting anomalies in plain English is a skill that takes time. AI can draft those explanations to a standard that's accurate, readable, and on-brand — you review and send. It's not writing for you. It's writing the first draft so you're not starting from a blank page at 6pm.

## How Accountants and Bookkeepers Use AI to Save Time: The Tools Worth Using

### ChatGPT (with a custom prompt library)

**Cost:** Free (GPT-4o access included); ChatGPT Plus at $20/month (approx. £16) for higher usage limits, file uploads, and access to more advanced models

**Best for:** Drafting client-facing narrative summaries, writing explanatory emails, turning technical journal entries into plain-English client updates, and answering "how do I explain this to my client" questions quickly

**Honest limitation:** ChatGPT has no direct connection to your accounting software. You'll need to copy and paste figures or upload a document manually. It also won't know your client's specific history unless you tell it — every session starts fresh unless you build a custom GPT with saved instructions for your practice.

---

### Copilot for Microsoft 365

**Cost:** From £25 per user per month (requires a qualifying Microsoft 365 Business subscription — not included in standard plans)

**Best for:** Practices already running on Word, Excel, and Outlook. Copilot can summarise long email threads, draft replies, generate Excel formulas from plain-English descriptions ("calculate the variance between these two columns as a percentage"), and convert meeting notes into structured action points without you lifting a finger.

**Honest limitation:** This is only worth the monthly cost if your whole team is already embedded in the Microsoft ecosystem. If you're running on Google Workspace or a Mac-first setup, the integration friction will cost you more time than the tool saves.

---

### Notion AI

**Cost:** Notion AI is an add-on at $10/month per user (approx. £8); base Notion plan starts free, with paid plans from $10/month per user

**Best for:** Building and maintaining your internal knowledge base — SOPs, client onboarding checklists, process documentation, and training materials. Notion AI can turn a set of bullet points into a formatted new client onboarding pack in minutes, or tidy up messy internal notes into a procedure document your team can actually follow.

**Honest limitation:** It's a writing and organisation tool, not a financial one. Don't expect it to understand accounting-specific concepts unless you prompt it clearly and give it the context it needs. It works best when you treat it as an intelligent drafting assistant for internal documentation — not a subject-matter expert.

---

### Fireflies.ai

**Cost:** Free plan available (limited transcription storage and features); Pro plan at $18/month per user (approx. £14)

**Best for:** Recording and transcribing client calls, then automatically generating a summary with key action points. If you've ever re-listened to a forty-minute onboarding call because you couldn't remember whether the client said their VAT registration date was January or February, this solves that problem immediately and permanently.

**Honest limitation:** Transcription accuracy drops on calls with heavy background noise or strong regional accents — and in accounting, one misheard date or figure matters. Always review the transcript before sending a summary to a client or relying on it for any compliance-related record. Integration with Zoom and Google Meet is solid, but Microsoft Teams integration can behave unpredictably depending on your organisation's settings.

---

### Dext (formerly Receipt Bank)

**Cost:** Pricing starts from approximately £19/month for sole practitioners and small practices; scales up with client volume and feature requirements

**Best for:** Automating the extraction of data from receipts, invoices, and bank statements. Clients photograph a receipt on their phone, it hits Dext, and the line-item data is extracted and pushed toward your accounting software with minimal manual entry. For practices with high receipt volumes, this alone can save several hours a week.

**Honest limitation:** Dext still makes extraction errors — particularly on handwritten receipts, foreign currency documents, or photos taken in poor lighting. You cannot treat its output as automatically correct. A review step needs to be built into your process from day one, not bolted on after something goes wrong.

---

### Otter.ai

**Cost:** Free plan available (limited to 300 minutes of transcription per month); Pro plan at $16.99/month per user (approx. £13); Business plan at $30/month per user

**Best for:** A slightly more affordable alternative to Fireflies for smaller practices that primarily need meeting transcription rather than a full call intelligence suite. Otter also integrates with Zoom and Google Meet and produces real-time transcripts you can annotate during the call itself — useful for complex client discussions where you want to highlight key moments as they happen.

**Honest limitation:** Otter's AI summary quality is good but not as detailed as Fireflies on longer, multi-topic calls. If you're running complex advisory sessions rather than short check-ins, Fireflies tends to produce more structured, usable summaries. Otter is the better starting point for a sole practitioner on a tighter budget.

## How to Get Started (Assume You've Never Done This Before)

1. **Pick your single biggest time drain this week.** Don't try to overhaul everything at once. Is it writing client update emails? Summarising monthly reports? Documenting your own processes? Choose one task and only one.

2. **Start with ChatGPT — it costs nothing to test.** Go to chat.openai.com, create a free account, and paste in the last client summary email you wrote. Ask it to write a similar one based on new figures you provide. See if the output is usable with light edits before you invest any money.

3. **Build a simple prompt you'll reuse every month.** Once you find something that works, save it — a Notes file, a Word document, anywhere you'll find it again. A prompt like: *"You are writing a plain-English monthly summary for a small business owner. Here are the figures: [paste figures here]. Explain the key movements in two short paragraphs, no jargon, no technical terms."* That's a prompt you can use every month with no extra thinking required.

4. **Add one more tool only when the first is routine.** Once client summaries are flowing faster, look at Fireflies or Otter for call notes, or Dext if receipt chasing is your next biggest problem. Adding tools before the first one is working is how you end up abandoning all of them.

5. **Build a review step into every AI-assisted output — without exception.** AI drafts, you approve. In a regulated profession this is not optional. A thirty-second check before anything touches a client is the minimum standard you should hold yourself to, every time.

## Realistic Expectations: What This Actually Looks Like

**Setup time:** Getting comfortable enough with ChatGPT to use it for drafting takes an afternoon of experimenting — not a course, not a consultant, just you and a blank prompt box. Building a reusable prompt library that covers your most common client communications takes a few weeks of iteration. Dext onboarding across a client base takes longer — budget a full working day per small practice, more if client receipt habits are inconsistent.

**When you'll see results:** Client narrative summaries will improve within your first week if you invest time in building one solid prompt. Fireflies or Otter pays back within the first call you actually need to reference — you'll feel it the first time you search for a specific figure instead of re-listening for twenty minutes. Dext's return depends entirely on your current manual input volume: the higher it is, the faster the hours stack up in your favour.

**What can go wrong:** The most common failure is using AI output without properly reviewing it. In accounting, one wrong figure or one misleading sentence in a client-facing document can damage trust faster than the time saved was worth. The second most common failure is trying to implement four tools simultaneously and feeling overwhelmed enough to abandon all of them by week three. Start narrow, go deep on one thing, then expand.

## How Accountants and Bookkeepers Use AI to Save Time: The Bottom Line

The point isn't to do less work — it's to stop spending your expertise on tasks that don't need it. Writing the first draft of a client email, formatting a report narrative, transcribing a call: none of that requires your qualification, your experience, or your professional judgement. AI handles the draft. You handle the decision.

Start this week with one workflow, one tool, and one prompt. Pick the task you're most tired of repeating, open ChatGPT for free, and spend twenty minutes testing whether it can produce something that's 80% of the way there. That 80% — reviewed and signed off by you — is still faster, and less draining, than starting from scratch every single time.
