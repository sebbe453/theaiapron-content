That's not magic. It's just a faster first draft — for free.

### Google Gemini (Free)

**Cost:** Free
**Best for:** Drafting longer content — think in-store event announcements, supplier emails, or a promotional blog post for your website. Also useful for summarising information quickly, like turning a product spec sheet into something a customer would actually want to read.

**Real example:** You're hosting a late-night shopping event before Christmas. You describe the date, offers, and vibe to Gemini and ask for a Facebook event description and a short email to your mailing list. Both done in under five minutes.

**Honest limitation:** Gemini can sometimes be overly formal or slightly generic in tone. You'll almost always need to add your shop's personality — a specific detail, a local reference, your own turn of phrase — before it sounds like you. Think of it as a first draft engine, not a finished copy machine.

### Canva Magic Write (Free Plan)

**Cost:** Free (Magic Write is included in Canva's free tier with limited monthly uses)
**Best for:** Writing short captions, taglines, and text content directly inside your design. If you're already using Canva to make your social graphics or promotional flyers, this keeps everything in one place.

**Real example:** You're making a graphic for a weekend sale. You've got the design ready. Instead of switching tabs to write the caption separately, you use Magic Write inside Canva to generate a few caption options while you're still looking at the image. It saves the back-and-forth.

**Honest limitation:** The free plan limits how many times you can use Magic Write per month (typically around 25 uses). For heavy content weeks, you'll use it up quickly. Canva Pro at £10.99/month removes that cap and adds more AI features, but the free version is genuinely useful if you're selective.

### Tidio (Free Plan)

**Cost:** Free for up to 50 conversations per month
**Best for:** Adding a live chat or basic chatbot to your website to answer common questions — opening hours, returns policy, product availability, local delivery radius — without you having to be available every hour.

**Real example:** A customer lands on your website at 9pm on a Tuesday and wants to know if you offer gift wrapping. Your Tidio chatbot answers immediately from a list of FAQs you set up once. The customer gets what they need. You're at home. Nobody had to do anything in real time.

**Honest limitation:** The free plan caps at 50 conversations per month, which is fine for quieter periods but will run out fast in December or around a big sale. The first paid tier starts at around £19/month if you need to scale up. Also, setting up the initial FAQ responses takes an hour or two upfront — it's not instant.

### Mailchimp (Free Plan) with AI Suggestions

**Cost:** Free for up to 500 contacts and 1,000 emails per month
**Best for:** Email marketing to your existing customers, with AI subject line suggestions built in. If you have a mailing list and aren't using it regularly, this is the lowest-effort way to start.

**Real example:** You want to send a quick email about your new spring arrivals. Mailchimp's AI suggests five subject line options based on your content. You pick the one most likely to get opened, schedule the email for Tuesday morning, and that's it. No agency. No copywriter. No extra cost.

**Honest limitation:** Mailchimp's AI features are modest compared to paid competitors — it's mainly subject line help and send-time suggestions, not full copy generation. You'll still need to write the body of your email, though ChatGPT handles that part well. The free plan also puts a Mailchimp badge in your footer, which some retailers find looks less professional.

## How to Get Started With Free AI Tools for Your Retail Business

1. **Pick one task that's eating your time.** Writing product descriptions, replying to reviews, or planning social posts are the easiest wins. Don't try to do everything at once.

2. **Open ChatGPT for free at chat.openai.com.** Create an account with your email address. No payment details needed for the free plan.

3. **Describe what you need in plain sentences.** Don't use jargon. Say: *"I sell vintage homewares in Bristol. Write three Instagram captions for a new range of retro glassware."* That's enough.

4. **Read the output, change anything that doesn't sound like you, and use it.** The goal isn't perfection — it's getting something done in a fraction of the time.

5. **Once ChatGPT feels comfortable, add one more tool.** Mailchimp for your emails, or Tidio if you have a website and get regular enquiries. Layer them in slowly.

## Realistic Expectations: Setup Time, Results, and What Can Go Wrong

**Setup time:** ChatGPT and Gemini take about five minutes to get started. Tidio takes an hour or two to set up properly because you need to write the FAQ answers it will give customers. Mailchimp takes thirty minutes to import your contact list and set up your first template.

**When you'll see results:** The time saving is immediate — most retailers notice it within the first week of using AI for writing tasks. Email open rates and social engagement take longer to judge; give it four to six weeks of consistent use before drawing conclusions.

**What can go wrong:** The biggest mistake is posting AI-generated content without reading it first. AI doesn't know your shop, your neighbourhood, or your voice. It gives you a draft. You make it real. Skipping that step is how you end up with copy that sounds like it came from a warehouse catalogue rather than a shop someone loves.

Also: free plans have limits. If you're in a busy period — Christmas, Valentine's Day, a local event — you may hit usage caps at exactly the wrong moment. Keep that in mind and batch your content work before the rush, not during it.
