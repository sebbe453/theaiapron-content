# How Opticians Use AI to Save Time on the Work That Fills Your Evenings and Empties Your Appointment Book

If you're losing 45 minutes a day to recall letters, rescheduling no-shows, and writing up clinical notes, AI can give most of that back — without replacing a single member of your team.

---

## The Real Problem Isn't the Eye Tests — It's Everything Around Them

The appointment itself takes 20–30 minutes. But the work around it? That's where your day disappears.

Recall campaigns that need updating. Patients who don't respond. No-shows that leave gaps you can't fill in time. Clinical notes that follow you home. Dispensing queries that bounce between you and the lab. Most of this work is predictable, repetitive, and exactly the kind of thing AI handles well.

The challenge for most independent opticians isn't knowing AI exists — it's knowing which parts of your day it can actually fix.

---

## What AI Actually Does for Opticians

AI doesn't examine eyes. What it does is take the surrounding admin — the communication, the documentation, the scheduling — and run it faster than any practice manager working alone can manage.

A realistic example: a two-chair independent practice in Manchester started using an AI-assisted recall system in early 2026. Within six weeks, their recall response rate had increased from 34% to 61%, simply because the system was sending the right message to the right patient at the right time, and following up automatically when there was no response. That's not magic — that's consistent, personalised outreach that a busy front desk can't always deliver manually.

On the clinical side, AI transcription tools can listen to an eye examination in real time and draft your notes before you've even finished washing your hands. You review, correct, and confirm — instead of typing from scratch at 6pm.

---

## How Opticians Use AI to Improve Recall Rates

This is where the financial return is clearest. Recall is the lifeblood of an optical practice, and most practices lose patients not because they move or stop caring about their eyesight — but because the reminder arrived at the wrong time, in the wrong format, or didn't arrive at all.

AI tools can segment your patient list by when they're actually due, what communication channel they've responded to before, and whether they've ignored a previous message. Then they send recalls automatically — by text, email, or letter — and follow up without anyone in your team having to think about it.

The result isn't just more appointments booked. It's fewer gaps, more consistent revenue, and a front desk that's chasing exceptions rather than running the whole recall list by hand.

**Pabau**

Request a specific quote for your practice size — the base plan and the full recall automation module are priced separately, so confirm exactly what's included before you sign up.
- **Best for:** Independent and small group opticians who want automated recalls, appointment reminders, and patient communication running without manual input each week. It's designed for clinical businesses, so it understands the logic of due dates, overdue patients, and multi-step outreach sequences.
- **Honest limitation:** Setup takes longer than the sales process suggests — expect two to three weeks to configure your recall rules properly before it runs as intended. It also works best when your existing patient data is clean. If your records have inconsistent contact details or outdated due dates, you'll spend meaningful time sorting that before the automation can do its job.

**Opticommerce / Optinet (integrated recall modules)**
- **Cost:** Typically bundled within the broader Optinet practice management licence. Recall module pricing varies — ask your account manager for the specific add-on cost rather than assuming it's included in your current plan.
- **Best for:** Practices already running on Optinet who want to add recall automation without switching platforms or migrating patient data. If your team already knows the system, the learning curve is minimal.

It will send recalls on time, but it won't adapt based on which channel a patient previously responded to or re-prioritise patients who've gone longest without contact. For many practices that's perfectly adequate — just don't expect it to behave like a dedicated marketing tool.

### 2. AI Clinical Note Drafting

**Heidi Health**
- **Cost:** Free plan available with a limited number of consultations per month. The Pro plan runs from approximately £29/month and removes usage caps, which is the version most full-time optometrists will need within a few weeks of starting.
- **Best for:** Optometrists who want to reduce the time spent writing up clinical notes after appointments. Heidi listens to the consultation in real time and produces a structured draft you review and confirm — rather than transcribing from memory at the end of a full day.
- **Honest limitation:** Heidi is designed for general clinical settings, not optical-specific documentation. You'll need to create your own note templates that match your preferred structure — it won't arrive knowing the difference between a routine sight test and a contact lens assessment. It also won't auto-populate your practice management software. For now, you'll copy the draft across manually, which adds a small step but still saves significant time versus writing from scratch.

### 3. Patient Communication and Enquiry Handling

**Tidio**
- **Cost:** A free tier is available and handles basic enquiry volumes. Paid plans start from approximately £19/month for the entry-level version, rising to around £49/month if you want the AI-assisted response features that go beyond scripted answers.
- **Best for:** Handling routine website enquiries — appointment requests, questions about frame brands, opening hours, contact lens product availability — without your team having to stop what they're doing to respond. Patients get an answer immediately; your front desk handles it when they have capacity.
- **Honest limitation:** Tidio handles common, predictable questions well. It struggles with anything specific to a patient's prescription history, dispensing order status, or clinical advice. You need a clear handoff process for anything account-specific or clinical — a message that says "a member of our team will contact you within one working day for anything related to your prescription" is enough, but it needs to be built into the setup from the start.

### 4. Content and Marketing Copy

**ChatGPT (via ChatGPT Plus)**
- **Cost:** £20/month. There is a free version, but the paid plan gives you access to more capable models and faster response times — for a business using it regularly, the difference in output quality justifies the cost.
- **Best for:** Writing recall email copy, practice newsletters, social media posts, frame promotion content, and responses to Google reviews. Anywhere you're currently staring at a blank page or recycling the same wording you used two years ago.
- **Honest limitation:** ChatGPT doesn't know your patients, your local area, or your brand tone until you tell it. If you ask it to "write a recall email," it will produce something generic. If you tell it "write a recall email for a two-chair independent optician in Bristol, warm and professional tone, for patients who are 12 months overdue and haven't responded to a previous text," the output is considerably more usable. A few sentences of context before each task makes a significant difference — it's a skill that takes a week to develop and then becomes fast.

## How Opticians Use AI: Getting Started With Zero Tech Experience

1. **Start with your recall problem, not the technology.** Write down how your current recall process works — who does what, when, and what consistently falls through the gaps. This tells you exactly what you need a tool to fix before you spend anything on it. A ten-minute conversation with your front desk is usually enough.

2. **Book a live demo with Pabau or your existing practice management provider.** Ask them specifically: "Can you show me how a recall sequence runs from trigger to confirmed appointment, including the automatic follow-up when a patient doesn't respond?" If they can't walk you through that live, you don't have enough information to make a decision.

3. **Pilot Heidi Health for one week of appointments.** Use the free plan to start. On day one, tell it your preferred note structure — the sections you always include, the abbreviations you use, the order you write things up. By day three, you'll have a clear sense of whether it fits your workflow, without any financial commitment.

4. **Use ChatGPT for the next piece of written content you actually need to produce.** A recall email, a frame promotion for your social channels, or a response to a recent Google review. Give it your practice name, location, and tone before you ask, then edit the output yourself. This step trains you to use it well, rather than treating it as a one-shot tool.

5. **Set a 30-day checkpoint.** At four weeks, ask yourself three questions: Is the recall tool sending the right messages to the right patients? Are note drafts saving me real time each day? Are routine website enquiries getting answered without my team stopping to handle them? If yes to all three, keep going. If not, something in the setup needs attention — not the technology itself.

## Realistic Expectations: Timeline, Results, and What Can Go Wrong

**Setup time:** Plan for two to four weeks before your recall automation is running properly. Most of that time isn't spent on the tool — it's reviewing your patient data, writing your recall rules clearly, and running test sequences before you go live with real patients.

**When results show:** Recall response rate improvements typically appear within four to six weeks, because you need a full outreach-and-follow-up cycle to complete before the numbers change. Note-writing time savings are visible from day one, but the quality of the drafts improves noticeably as you refine your templates through the first two weeks.

**What can go wrong:** The two most common problems are patient data quality and skipping configuration. Most opticians who find that "AI didn't work" went live with a tool before they'd set their own rules clearly. The tool does exactly what you instruct it to do — if those instructions are vague or based on messy data, the output reflects that.

One more honest point: AI will not fix a recall problem that's rooted in patient experience or pricing. If people aren't coming back because they had a poor experience or found the same frames significantly cheaper elsewhere, better-timed messaging won't change that. Stronger communication is only as effective as the experience it's inviting
