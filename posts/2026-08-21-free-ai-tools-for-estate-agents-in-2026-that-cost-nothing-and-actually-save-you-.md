## Free AI Tools for Estate Agents: What to Use and When

### ChatGPT (Free Tier)

**Cost:** Free (GPT-4o available on free plan as of 2026, with usage limits)

**Best for:** Property descriptions, email drafts, offer letters, price reduction scripts, vendor update emails

**How it works in practice:** You type something like: *"Write a Rightmove listing for a 3-bed semi in Lichfield, recently renovated kitchen, south-facing garden, close to the train station, target buyer is a young family"* — and you get a solid 150-word description in seconds. Edit the bits that don't sound like you, and it's ready.

**Honest limitation:** The free tier has session limits and won't remember your previous conversations. Every time you start a new session, you'll need to re-explain your tone preferences. Saving a short prompt template in your Notes app solves this in 30 seconds.

### Google Gemini (Free)

**Cost:** Free — no subscription required, no credit card needed

**Best for:** Summarising long email threads, drafting replies to tricky enquiries, writing social content, researching local area selling points

**How it works in practice:** If a buyer sends a long-winded email with seven questions buried in four paragraphs, paste it into Gemini and ask it to list the questions clearly and draft a reply. What would take 20 minutes of re-reading and responding takes about three. It also does a solid job of pulling together area information — schools, transport links, regeneration news — when you need to beef up a listing's lifestyle section.

**Honest limitation:** Gemini occasionally writes in a formal, slightly corporate tone that can feel stiff for local agency communications. You'll want to add "write this in a warm, friendly tone" to every prompt until you find a phrasing that consistently produces the style you want. It takes a few attempts, but once you've cracked your tone instruction, it becomes reliable.

---

### Claude (Free Tier — Anthropic)

**Cost:** Free (with daily message limits)

**Best for:** Longer documents — tenancy agreement summaries, letter templates, viewing feedback write-ups, landlord communications

**How it works in practice:** Claude handles longer, more nuanced content better than most free tools. If you need to write a sensitive letter to a vendor explaining a price reduction, Claude tends to produce drafts that sound measured and human rather than blunt or clinical. It's the tool to reach for when the writing needs to feel considered, not just competent.

**Honest limitation:** The free tier message limit can feel restrictive during busy periods. It's worth having ChatGPT open as a backup so you're not stuck when Claude hits its daily cap mid-morning.

### Canva (Free Tier)

**Cost:** Free (Pro version available at around £13/month but not required for most agents)

**Best for:** Property marketing graphics, social media posts, open day flyers, email headers, for-sale announcement posts

**How it works in practice:** Canva's free templates and basic AI text tools let you create a professional-looking "New Instruction" Instagram post in under five minutes. Search "estate agent Instagram post" in the template library, drop in your photo, swap the headline text, and it's done. No design skills needed, no software to install.

**Honest limitation:** The free tier limits access to some premium templates and Canva's AI image generation feature has monthly usage caps. For most estate agents posting three to four times a week, the free version covers everything you need — but if you want the full template library and unlimited AI features, you'll need to upgrade.

### Notion AI (Free Base / Paid AI Add-On)

**Cost:** Notion's base product is free for personal use. The AI add-on costs around £8/month. For template storage and prompt libraries — the main use case here — the free version is sufficient.

**Best for:** Building a library of reusable prompt templates, email scripts, and listing copy frameworks you return to every week

**How it works in practice:** Create a Notion page called "Listing Templates" and store your best ChatGPT prompts there — one for terraced houses, one for new builds, one for rental properties. Within two weeks, you'll have a system that means you never start from scratch again. It's less about AI and more about building a permanent, searchable toolkit.

**Honest limitation:** Notion has a learning curve if you've never used it. The interface is more flexible than most tools, which can feel overwhelming at first. If it feels like too much to set up, a simple Google Doc works just as well for storing templates — don't let the tool get in the way of the habit.

---

### Otter.ai (Free Tier)

**Cost:** Free (300 minutes of transcription per month on the free plan)

**Best for:** Recording and transcribing vendor valuations, buyer consultations, or team briefings — then turning the transcript into a follow-up email or action summary

**How it works in practice:** After a 45-minute valuation meeting, you have a full transcript waiting on your phone. Paste the key points into ChatGPT and ask it to write a follow-up email summarising the agreed asking price, the marketing plan, and the next steps. You're done in under five minutes, and the email is more accurate than anything you'd write from memory an hour later.

**Honest limitation:** Otter's free tier gives you 300 minutes per month — roughly six to seven hours of recordings. For most agents that's sufficient, but if you're running multiple long meetings every week, you'll hit the cap before the month is out. The paid plan starts at around £8/month if you need more capacity.

## How to Get Started With Free AI Tools for Estate Agents

1. **Start with one task you do every day.** Property descriptions are the best entry point. Open ChatGPT, type the details of your next listing — bedrooms, location, key features, target buyer — and add "write a 150-word Rightmove listing in a warm, engaging tone." Read what it gives you. You'll likely use 80% of it as-is.

2. **Save your best prompts immediately.** Open a Google Doc called "AI Prompts – [Your Agency Name]". Every time a prompt produces something you like, copy it in. Within a week you'll have a working toolkit that gets faster every time you use it.

3. **Use Gemini for email.** The next time you receive a long, messy enquiry with multiple questions buried in it, paste it into Gemini and ask it to summarise the questions and draft a reply. Note how much time that saves compared to your usual approach.

4. **Set up Canva for social.** Create a free account, search "estate agent Instagram post" in templates, and customise one for your next new instruction. Post it. That's your social presence sorted for the week, in under ten minutes.

5. **Try Otter at your next valuation.** Open the Otter app on your phone, hit record at the start of the meeting, and let it run. After the meeting, use the transcript to write your follow-up email. You'll never go back to writing it from memory.

## Realistic Expectations: Setup, Timeline, and What Can Go Wrong

**Setup time:** Getting started with all five tools takes about 90 minutes total. Most of that is creating accounts. The actual learning — typing prompts and reading outputs — happens in the first two or three real uses.

**When results show:** Most agents save 45 to 90 minutes per day within the first week of consistent use. Listing copy and email drafting are where the time savings are most immediate and most obvious.

**What can go wrong:** The biggest failure mode is quitting after one mediocre output. AI drafts are starting points, not finished work. If the first property description sounds generic, give it more detail — add the road name, the style of buyer you're targeting, a specific feature that makes the property unusual. Better input produces better output, every time.

There's also a real risk of over-relying on AI for communications that need local knowledge or emotional sensitivity. A vendor who's just had an offer fall through doesn't need an AI-drafted email — they need a human phone call. Use these tools for the routine and the repetitive. Keep the important moments personal.
