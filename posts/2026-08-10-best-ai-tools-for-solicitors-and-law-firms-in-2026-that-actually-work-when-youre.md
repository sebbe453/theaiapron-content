## What AI Actually Does for Solicitors and Law Firms

AI tools for law firms in 2026 sit in a practical middle ground: they don't give legal advice, and they shouldn't. What they do is handle the surrounding infrastructure — drafting routine client communications, summarising long documents, converting meeting notes into structured file notes, and generating first-draft letters that your fee earners review and send in two minutes instead of writing from scratch in twenty.

Think of it this way. A conveyancing solicitor handling fifteen active matters might spend forty minutes a day writing update emails that all say essentially the same thing in slightly different ways. An AI tool can draft all fifteen of those emails — personalised, professional, and accurate — in under five minutes, based on the status notes your team already has. The solicitor reads, adjusts if needed, and sends. The legal judgment stays entirely human. The typing doesn't.

Beyond client communication, AI tools are handling document review support, meeting transcription, precedent drafting assistance, and internal knowledge management. None of these require technical expertise to set up or use.

### For Client Communication and Email Drafting

**Clio Duo (within Clio Manage)**
- **Cost:** Included in Clio Manage plans from £79/user/month [Clio](https://www.google.com/search?q=Clio+pricing+review)
- **Best for:** Firms already using Clio who want AI-assisted drafting built directly into their practice management workflow — client emails, matter summaries, and file notes without switching between apps
- **Honest limitation:** Only useful if you're already on Clio. If you're not, the AI feature alone isn't a reason to switch — evaluate the full platform on its own merits first, not just for this feature

**ChatGPT (via OpenAI)**
- **Cost:** Free tier available; ChatGPT Plus at $20/month (~£16/month)
- **Best for:** Drafting client update letters, creating template communications, and rephrasing complex legal language into plain English for client-facing documents
- **Honest limitation:** Has no connection to your matter management system, so you provide context manually every time. It also doesn't know your firm's preferred tone unless you tell it — first outputs regularly need adjustment before they're ready to send

### For Meeting Notes and File Note Drafting

**Otter.ai**
- **Cost:** Free for 300 minutes/month; Pro at $16.99/month (~£13/month) [Otter.ai](https://www.google.com/search?q=Otter.ai+pricing+review)
- **Best for:** Recording client meetings or internal calls and automatically producing a structured transcript — which you then use to generate a clean file note in ChatGPT or paste directly into your case management system
- **Honest limitation:** Accuracy drops noticeably with strong regional accents, legal terminology, or poor audio quality. Always review the transcript before it becomes a file note — treat it as a first draft, not a finished document

**Fireflies.ai**
- **Cost:** Free for limited use; Pro at $18/month (~£14/month) per user
- **Best for:** Teams using Zoom or Microsoft Teams for client calls — Fireflies joins automatically, records, transcribes, and produces a searchable summary with action points flagged
- **Honest limitation:** Joining calls automatically can unsettle some clients if not mentioned beforehand. You'll need a clear disclosure process in your client care letter or at the start of every call — this isn't optional, it's a professional obligation

### For Document Review Support and Long-Text Summarisation

**Claude (by Anthropic)**
- **Cost:** Free tier available; Claude Pro at $20/month (~£16/month)
- **Best for:** Summarising long contracts, reports, or correspondence into a structured brief — particularly useful for fee earners preparing for a call who need the key points from a forty-page document in ten minutes rather than forty
- **Honest limitation:** Claude summarises and highlights — it does not provide legal analysis. Anything it flags as a potential issue still requires a qualified solicitor to assess. This is a reading assistant, not a legal opinion, and should never be positioned as one internally

**Spellbook (by Rally)**
- **Cost:** Starts at approximately $99/month (~£79/month) for individual users; team plans vary
- **Best for:** Contract review support — identifying standard and non-standard clauses, flagging missing provisions, and suggesting alternative drafting language for routine commercial contracts, all inside Microsoft Word
- **Honest limitation:** Works best on standard commercial contract types. Complex, bespoke, or highly technical agreements still require full fee earner attention from start to finish. Treat the output as a structured starting point, not a completed review

### For Internal Knowledge and Precedent Access

**Notion AI (within Notion)**
- **Cost:** Notion free tier available; AI add-on at $10/user/month (~£8/month) [Notion](https://www.google.com/search?q=Notion+pricing+review)

- **Honest limitation:** Only as good as what you put in. If your precedents and processes aren't documented anywhere in Notion, the AI has nothing to work from. Expect one to two days of setup time before it becomes genuinely useful, not ten minutes

## How to Get Started With AI Tools for Solicitors (No IT Department Required)

1. **Pick one task that eats your time.** Don't try to overhaul your whole workflow at once. Choose the single most repetitive task your team faces — client update emails, file notes after calls, or first-draft letters — and start there only.

2. **Try ChatGPT free for two weeks on that task alone.** Go to chat.openai.com, create a free account, and use this as your starting prompt: *"Write a client update email for a conveyancing matter. The matter is at exchange stage. The client is waiting to hear about a completion date. Tone should be professional and reassuring. Key facts: [add your details here]."* Adjust until the output matches your firm's standard, then save that prompt.

3. **Build a prompt library, not a dependency.** Once you find a version that works, save it in a shared document every team member can access. This becomes your firm's prompt library — consistent output for everyone, without starting from scratch each time or relying on one person who figured it out.

4. **Add a transcription tool to your next client call.** Download Otter.ai or Fireflies, connect it to your calendar or meeting platform, and review what the transcript looks like after a real call. Judge whether the accuracy meets your firm's standards before you rely on it for file notes.

5. **Review what's actually being used after thirty days.** If a tool is saving time, keep it. If a fee earner hasn't opened it once since the first week, drop it without ceremony. You're not building a technology stack — you're looking for tools that earn their place in the working day.

## Realistic Expectations: Setup Time, Timeline, and What Can Go Wrong

**Setup time:** For ChatGPT, you're operational in under ten minutes. For Otter.ai or Fireflies, allow thirty minutes to connect your calendar and run a test recording before a real client call. For Notion AI, expect one to two days of upfront work documenting your processes before the AI becomes genuinely useful rather than just interesting.

**When results show:** Most fee earners notice meaningful time savings within the first week of consistent AI-assisted drafting — but only if they use it daily, not as an occasional experiment. The habit matters more than the tool. A great tool used twice a month saves almost nothing.

**What can go wrong:** The biggest risk in a legal context isn't the technology failing — it's over-reliance. AI drafts must be reviewed before they reach clients. A poorly checked AI-generated letter with a factual error or the wrong tone still carries your firm's name on it. Build the review step into your process as standard, not as something left to the individual fee earner's judgment on a given day.

Data security is a real and serious concern here. Do not paste confidential client information into a free public AI tool. Use anonymised or illustrative details in prompts, or invest in tools that offer appropriate data processing agreements for professional use. Clio operates within a legal-specific data environment with relevant security standards. Free ChatGPT does not — and the distinction matters when your professional obligations are involved.
