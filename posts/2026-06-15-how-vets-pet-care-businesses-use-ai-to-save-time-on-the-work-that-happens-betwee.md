# How Vets and Pet Care Businesses Use AI to Save Time on the Work That Happens After the Consultation

---

The average vet spends 1.5 to 2 hours every day writing up clinical notes after appointments. That's time you're not billing for, not resting during, and not spending with the animals actually in front of you.

## Why Vets Are Drowning in Time They Can't Bill

The consult itself might last 15 minutes. But the work surrounding it — the notes, the follow-up calls, the vaccination reminders, the referral letters, the prescription queries — can easily double or triple that time before you've left the building.

Small practices are running on stretched staff, which means admin falls on clinical people who have better things to do with their training. The result is late evenings, burned-out teams, and a business that feels permanently reactive.

AI doesn't fix the workload. But it can take specific, repeatable tasks off your plate — fast enough to make a real difference by the end of the week.

---

## What AI Actually Does for Vets and Pet Care Businesses

The biggest time wins in veterinary practice right now are in documentation and communication. AI tools can listen to a consultation (with client consent), generate a clinical note draft in under a minute, and flag anything you need to check or add. Instead of starting from a blank screen at 7pm, you're reviewing and approving a draft that's already 80% done.

For pet care businesses — kennels, groomers, pet sitters — the time drain looks different but the fix is similar. You're writing booking confirmations, answering the same questions about availability and pricing, following up on missed enquiries, and trying to keep existing clients coming back. AI handles the writing and the chasing so you can focus on the animals.

---

## How Vets Use AI to Save Time on Clinical Notes

This is where the numbers get specific. Practices using AI transcription and note-drafting tools report saving 20 to 40 minutes per vet per day on documentation alone. Across a five-day week, that's three to four hours back — per person.

Tools designed for veterinary use understand clinical terminology, generate SOAP-format notes (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan), and can flag incomplete entries. You still review everything. But you're editing, not writing from scratch.

**Tool: Talkatoo**
- **Cost:** From $149/month per user
- **Best for:** Vets who want voice dictation built specifically for clinical vocabulary — it understands terms like "cruciates," "pyoderma," and "melena" without needing manual correction
- **Honest limitation:** Talkatoo is a dictation tool, not a full AI note-writer. You still need to speak your notes out loud; it captures and formats them faster than typing, but the input still comes from you. It works best when you dictate immediately after a consult, not from memory hours later

**Tool: Vetted AI (integrated within practice management software)**
- **Cost:** Pricing varies by practice management system — typically $50–$120/month as an add-on, depending on your platform
- **Best for:** Practices already using platforms like ezyVet or Provet Cloud who want AI note drafts generated directly from consultation transcripts
- **Honest limitation:** Output quality depends heavily on audio clarity. A noisy consultation room — barking in the background, equipment running — will produce rougher drafts that need significantly more editing before they're usable

## How Vets Use AI to Save Time on Client Communication

Vaccination reminders. Post-op care instructions. Appointment confirmations. Prescription renewal prompts. These messages go out dozens of times a week and almost none of them need a human to write them individually each time.

AI tools connected to your practice management system can generate and send these automatically — personalised with the pet's name, the correct medication, and the right follow-up date. A reminder for a cat's annual booster reads differently from one for a dog's post-surgical check, and the AI handles that distinction without you touching it.

**Tool: Digitail**
- **Cost:** From $149/month for independent practices
- **Best for:** Automated client communication — reminders, post-visit follow-ups, health plan renewals — with built-in AI drafting for personalised messages at scale
- **Honest limitation:** Setup takes real time upfront. You need to map out your communication workflows before the automation can mirror them accurately. Plan for half a day of configuration, not a quick plug-and-play. Skipping this step produces generic, inconsistent messages that clients notice

**Tool: ChatGPT (via OpenAI) or Claude (via Anthropic)**
- **Cost:** Free tier available for both; paid plans from $20/month per user
- **Best for:** Writing template messages, client FAQs, post-op care instruction sheets, and referral letter drafts that your team then personalises before sending
- **Honest limitation:** Both are general-purpose tools. They don't connect to your patient records, so you're copying and pasting client information manually rather than automating anything end-to-end. Useful for drafting, not for hands-free sending

## How Pet Care Businesses Use AI to Save Time on Bookings and Enquiries

A busy boarding kennel can receive 30 to 50 enquiries a week during peak season. Answering each one manually — availability checks, pricing breakdowns, vaccination requirements — eats hours that don't generate revenue directly.

An AI chat tool on your website can handle the first response to every enquiry, 24 hours a day. It answers common questions, qualifies the booking (does the dog have up-to-date vaccinations? Is this for boarding or daycare?), and only passes genuinely complex queries to a human. One kennel owner using this setup reported cutting her enquiry response time from "hours or the next morning" to under two minutes — which directly improved her booking conversion rate because faster replies win more bookings.

**Tool: Tidio**
- **Cost:** Free plan available; Tidio+ from $29/month
- **Best for:** Pet care businesses wanting a website chatbot that answers FAQs, collects booking details, and follows up on abandoned enquiries automatically around the clock
- **Honest limitation:** You need to write the initial responses and Q&A logic yourself before the AI can use them. A thin setup — three questions and vague answers — produces thin, unhelpful results. The tool is only as good as what you put into it at the start

**Tool: Notion AI (within Notion)**
- **Cost:** Notion free plan plus AI add-on from $10/month per user
- **Best for:** Groomers, pet sitters, and small kennels who want to build a simple client FAQ base and generate response templates without committing to dedicated customer service software
- **Honest limitation:** This is not a chatbot and doesn't automate outbound messages or live website interactions. It helps you write and organise content — which you then copy and paste or paste into other tools. A useful writing assistant, not an automation platform

## How to Get Started (Zero Tech Knowledge Required)

1. **Pick one task that's eating your time this week** — clinical notes, vaccination reminders, or booking enquiries. Don't try to fix everything at once or you'll set up three tools badly instead of one tool well.

2. **Start with a general AI tool you already have access to** — if you haven't used ChatGPT, create a free account and spend 20 minutes asking it to draft one post-op care instruction sheet. See how close it gets without any input from you beyond a basic description. That's your baseline for what's possible.

3. **If clinical notes are your biggest drain, trial Talkatoo** — most AI dictation tools for vets offer a free trial period. Use it for one full week on real consultations and time how long note-writing takes compared to your usual process. If you don't see a difference in week one, check whether you're dictating immediately after each consult or leaving it until later.

4. **If client communication is the problem, check what you're already paying for** — log into your existing practice management platform and look for automated messaging features you haven't turned on yet. Many practices are sitting on tools they're already paying for but have never configured. Before buying anything new, spend 30 minutes exploring what's already there.

5. **Set a 30-day review point before you commit to anything** — write down how long specific tasks take before you start. After 30 days, check the same tasks again. If you haven't saved measurable time, either the tool needs better setup or it's not the right fit for how your practice actually runs.

## Realistic Expectations: Timeline, Results, and What Can Go Wrong

**Setup time:** A dictation tool like Talkatoo takes around 30 minutes to get running for the first time. Automated communication workflows in a platform like Digitail take closer to half a day if you're building them properly. A website chatbot through Tidio sits somewhere in between — an hour to get live, but several more hours to make the responses genuinely useful.

**When you'll see results:** Most practices notice a measurable difference in daily note time within the first week of consistent AI dictation use. Client communication improvements take two to four weeks — you need enough automated messages to go out before the time saving becomes obvious.

**What can go wrong:** The most common failure is setting up a tool and then using it inconsistently. AI dictation only saves you time if you dictate after every consult — not just when you remember to. Automation only works if your workflows are clearly mapped out first. And no AI tool will fix a process that was already broken before you introduced it. If your vaccination reminder system is a mess manually, automating it produces a faster mess.
