# How Wedding Planners Use AI to Save Time on the Work That Happens Between Client Meetings (Not Just the Planning)

You spent four hours last Tuesday writing emails. Not planning weddings — writing emails. Chasing suppliers, responding to "quick questions," sending the same venue checklist you've sent 47 times.

## The Real Time Problem Isn't the Planning — It's Everything Around It

Wedding planning looks creative from the outside. From the inside, it's a mountain of repetitive communication, document management, and admin that has nothing to do with why you got into this work.

Most of the AI content aimed at wedding planners talks about generating mood boards or writing social captions. That's not where your hours are disappearing. The drain is in the in-between: the follow-ups, the first drafts, the scheduling, the supplier confirmations, the client updates that need to be warm but also accurate and also done by 9pm.

That's exactly where AI earns its keep — not as a creative substitute, but as the thing that handles the repeatable, time-consuming work so you can focus on the clients in front of you.

## What AI Actually Does for Wedding Planners Day to Day

AI handles the communication volume that comes with running multiple weddings simultaneously. When you have six couples at different stages — two in early planning, two in the thick of vendor coordination, two in final-month logistics — the emails alone can swallow half your week. AI writing tools let you draft a detailed, personalised-sounding update email in under three minutes instead of twenty.

It also handles the document work that never gets talked about. Budget trackers, run-of-day timelines, venue briefing docs, supplier contact sheets — these take hours to build from scratch each time. AI can generate a working first draft from a prompt, which you then edit. That's still editing, not writing from zero, and the difference in time is significant.

Where it's honest about its limits: AI doesn't know your clients, your suppliers, or the specific venue. It writes first drafts, not finished work. You still need to read, adjust, and make it human. The judgment calls — handling a nervous bride, managing a supplier who's gone quiet, reading the room with a family who's disagreeing about the guest list — those are yours, full stop.

## Which AI Tools Are Actually Worth Using for Wedding Planners (With Real Pricing)

### For Client Communication and Email Drafting

**ChatGPT (OpenAI)**
- **Cost:** Free tier available; ChatGPT Plus costs £20/month
- **Best for:** Drafting client update emails, supplier follow-ups, FAQ responses, and run-of-day timelines from bullet points
- **Honest limitation:** It writes in a generic voice until you train it on your own style. Spend 30 minutes giving it examples of your existing emails and the quality improves significantly. Without that groundwork, everything sounds like it came from the same template.

### For Scheduling and Booking Automation

**Calendly**
- **Cost:** Free for basic; Standard plan is £10/month
- **Best for:** Replacing the back-and-forth of booking discovery calls, check-in meetings, and venue visits. Clients book directly into your calendar based on your real availability, without a single email exchange.
- **Honest limitation:** It doesn't handle rescheduling conversations well. If a client cancels repeatedly or needs to explain their situation before booking, you're still managing that manually. It solves the logistics of scheduling, not the human side of it.

## How to Get Started With AI as a Wedding Planner (From Zero)

**Step 1: Pick one email you write every single week.**
This could be the "here's your planning update" email, the venue confirmation, or the supplier briefing. Choose the one you write most often. That's your starting point — not a grand system, just one repeated task.

**Step 2: Open ChatGPT (free account, no card needed) and paste in an example.**
Type: *"Here is an example of an email I send to clients. Learn my tone and help me write future versions."* Paste your example. Then give it a new brief and see what it produces.

**Step 3: Edit the output and note what needs changing.**
Don't accept the first draft. Adjust it, then add those corrections into your original instructions. You're building a better prompt over time, not waiting for a perfect tool to appear.

**Step 4: Once it's working for emails, move to documents.**
Ask it to generate a run-of-day timeline from your bullet points, or a first-draft supplier briefing from a list of requirements. These tasks are where the real time savings accumulate over a full week.

**Step 5: Set up Calendly for your booking calls.**
This takes about 45 minutes to configure properly. Connect it to your calendar, set your availability windows, and put the booking link in your email signature and website enquiry confirmation. Most planners stop playing email tennis for appointment slots within the first week.

## Realistic Expectations: When Does It Actually Pay Off?

**Setup time:** A working ChatGPT prompt takes about an hour to develop properly across two or three short sessions. Calendly takes one focused afternoon. HoneyBook takes two to three sessions before it's genuinely useful — expect a setup period of two to three weeks before the time savings feel real.

**When you'll feel the difference:** Most wedding planners notice a meaningful reduction in communication time within two to three weeks of using AI drafting consistently. It's not instant — you need to train your prompts and build the habit of reaching for the tool before opening a blank email.

**What can go wrong:** The biggest trap is accepting AI output without reading it carefully. A rushed AI-drafted email with an error — wrong date, wrong venue name, wrong supplier contact — costs more time to fix, and more client trust, than the email would have taken to write yourself. Read everything before it leaves your outbox.

The other risk is tool overload. Don't try to set up four things at once. Start with one, use it consistently for two weeks, then add the next. The temptation to build the whole system on a Sunday afternoon is real, but it leads to half-configured tools you stop using by Wednesday.

If you're spending more than two hours a week writing the same types of emails and documents, AI writing tools will give you at least half of that time back — not eventually, but within a few weeks of consistent use.
