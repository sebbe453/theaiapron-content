# How Mortgage Brokers Use AI to Save Time on the Work That Slows Down Every Case (Before the Lender Even Sees It)

Your average mortgage case takes 6–8 hours of broker time — and at least half of that happens before the lender gets involved. That's the problem worth solving.

---

## The Work Nobody Talks About (But Everyone Feels)

Ask any broker where the time actually goes and they'll tell you the same thing: it's not the advice conversation, it's everything around it.

Chasing documents. Re-explaining the same process to anxious first-time buyers at 9pm. Writing suitability letters that all say roughly the same thing with slightly different numbers. Cross-referencing criteria across lenders because your sourcing software doesn't tell you *why* a case might fall over — just whether it passes.

That's not mortgage broking. That's administration dressed up as mortgage broking. And AI is actually quite good at taking that off your plate.

---

## What AI Actually Does Here

AI doesn't replace your judgment on a complex case. It removes the repetitive scaffolding around it so you can focus on the part that needs a human.

Specifically: AI can draft your suitability letters from a case summary in under two minutes. It can handle the 11pm "what happens next?" message from a nervous buyer. It can help you build a document checklist tailored to a client's employment type before your first meeting. None of this is magic — it's just a faster way to do the low-value work that currently fills your evenings.

The bigger win is consistency. When you're doing five cases at once, AI tools help you maintain the same standard on case five as you did on case one — without burning out.

## The Tools Worth Using in 2026

### ChatGPT (with a custom system prompt)
**Cost:** Free (GPT-4o) or £16/month for ChatGPT Plus
**Best for:** Drafting suitability letters, writing client explainer emails, building case summaries, creating first-draft scripts for client objection handling
**Honest limitation:** It doesn't know your specific lender criteria or your client's actual file. You need to feed it the right information — it won't pull from your CRM automatically. Treat it like a very fast junior writer who needs a good brief.

[ChatGPT](https://www.google.com/search?q=ChatGPT+pricing+review)

---

### Copilot (Microsoft 365)
**Cost:** £25/user/month (included in Microsoft 365 Business Standard with the Copilot add-on)
**Best for:** Brokers already working in Word, Outlook, and Teams. Drafts suitability letters directly inside Word, summarises email threads, and can pull talking points from a client document you've pasted in.
**Honest limitation:** Only useful if you're already in the Microsoft ecosystem. If your workflow lives in Google or a specialist CRM, this adds friction rather than removing it.

[Microsoft Copilot](https://www.google.com/search?q=Microsoft+Copilot+pricing+review)

---

### Tidio AI (Lyro)
**Cost:** Free tier available for basic live chat; Lyro AI from £29/month
**Best for:** Handling after-hours client enquiries via your website. First-time buyers especially ask the same questions repeatedly — "How long does it take?", "What documents do I need?", "Can I get a mortgage with a CCJ?" Lyro answers these from a knowledge base you build once, so you're not fielding the same WhatsApp message at 10pm.
**Honest limitation:** Tidio cannot give regulated mortgage advice, and it will need careful setup to avoid any response straying into advice territory. You must build a compliance-aware disclaimer into every automated reply — something like "This is an automated response for general information only. For regulated mortgage advice, please speak with a qualified broker." Do not skip this step. It is not optional.

[Visit Tidio](https://www.tidio.com)

---

### Otter.ai
**Cost:** Free plan (300 minutes/month); Pro at £8.33/month
**Best for:** Transcribing client calls and fact-find meetings. After a call, Otter gives you a searchable transcript so you're not relying on handwritten notes when writing the suitability letter later. One broker using this reported cutting their post-call admin time from 45 minutes to around 15.
**Honest limitation:** Accuracy drops with strong accents, background noise, or when two people speak over each other — which happens regularly on joint mortgage calls. Always review the transcript before using it as a reference.

[Otter.ai](https://www.google.com/search?q=Otter.ai+pricing+review)

---

### Notion AI
**Cost:** Notion free with limited AI; Notion AI add-on at £8/user/month
**Best for:** Building a living knowledge base of your lender criteria, referral scripts, compliance notes, and process templates — with AI that can search and summarise across it instantly. Particularly useful if you run a small team and need everyone working from the same up-to-date information.
**Honest limitation:** Takes real setup time to get value from. If you don't commit two or three hours to building the knowledge base properly, it's just an expensive notebook. This is a medium-term investment, not a quick win.

[Notion](https://www.google.com/search?q=Notion+pricing+review)

## How to Get Started With AI as a Mortgage Broker

**Step 1: Start with suitability letters.**
Open ChatGPT (the free version is fine to begin). Paste in an anonymised case summary — employment type, income, deposit, property value, any complications. Type: *"Write a suitability letter for a mortgage broker in the UK using this case. Keep it professional and factual."* Review what comes back. It won't be perfect, but it will be 80% there in under two minutes.

**Step 2: Build a document checklist prompt.**
Ask ChatGPT: *"What documents does a self-employed sole trader typically need for a UK mortgage application?"* Save that response and refine it over time. That becomes a reusable template you reach for at the start of every self-employed case.

**Step 3: Set up Otter.ai for your next client call.**
Download the app. Record your next fact-find call with client consent. After the call, read the transcript while it's fresh and compare it to your handwritten notes. After three calls, you'll have a clear sense of whether it earns its place in your workflow.

**Step 4: Add Tidio to your website for after-hours enquiries.**
Install the free version. In the knowledge base, write answers to your ten most common client questions. Enable the AI to respond when you're offline, and make sure every automated reply includes a clear statement that it is not regulated advice.

**Step 5: Track what you're no longer doing manually.**
After 30 days, count how many suitability letter drafts you started from AI, how many after-hours messages the chatbot handled, and how many calls you didn't need to re-listen to because you had a transcript. That's your actual return — and it's usually enough to justify the cost of the paid tools.

## Realistic Expectations: Timeline, Setup, and What Can Go Wrong

**Setup time:** ChatGPT and Otter.ai are usable within an hour. Tidio takes a weekend to configure properly if you want it compliance-ready. Notion AI is a three-to-four week project if you're building a knowledge base worth having.

**When you'll see results:** Suitability letter drafting pays back on the first use. Client communication tools like Tidio take two to three weeks before the knowledge base is strong enough to handle real queries confidently without you needing to step in.

**What can go wrong:**

AI-drafted letters sound generic if you give them a vague brief. The more specific your input, the better the output. Always proof for accuracy — AI will occasionally invent plausible-sounding figures or miss a nuance in your client's situation.

Compliance remains your responsibility, not the tool's. No AI tool currently holds FCA authorisation. Everything it produces needs your professional review before it reaches a client.

Client-facing chatbots need guardrails built in from the start. A response that drifts into personalised advice — even inadvertently — creates real risk. Build your knowledge base around process and general information, not product recommendations or personalised guidance.
