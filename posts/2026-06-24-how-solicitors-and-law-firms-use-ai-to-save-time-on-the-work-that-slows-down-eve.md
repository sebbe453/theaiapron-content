# How Solicitors and Law Firms Use AI to Save Time on the Work That Slows Down Every Case (Not Just the Paperwork)

If you billed every hour you actually worked last week, you'd have invoiced significantly more than you did. The gap between hours worked and hours billed is where most law firms quietly lose money — and AI is closing it.

---

## The Real Problem Isn't Workload. It's Unbillable Time.

Most solicitors aren't struggling because they don't have enough clients. They're struggling because a huge chunk of every working day disappears into tasks that don't appear on any invoice: chasing documents, summarising long files, drafting standard correspondence, formatting precedents, and updating clients who just want to know where things stand.

A small family law firm running two fee earners might spend 12 to 15 hours a week on exactly this kind of work. That's not a staffing problem. That's a systems problem — and it's one AI tools can genuinely address right now, without a technical background or a costly IT project.

---

## What AI Actually Does for Solicitors (In Plain English)

AI won't give legal advice, appear in court for you, or replace a trained fee earner. What it does is take the repetitive cognitive work — reading, summarising, drafting, formatting, responding — and compress it into minutes rather than hours.

A conveyancing solicitor handling 20 files at once doesn't need AI to do the conveyancing. She needs AI to read a 40-page report and pull out the three things that matter, draft the client update email, and flag the clause that needs her attention. That's exactly the kind of task modern AI handles well, consistently, and quickly.

The more structured and repeatable the task, the more useful AI becomes. Legal work has plenty of both.

## How Solicitors and Law Firms Are Using AI to Save Time Right Now

### Drafting and Editing Letters, Emails, and Standard Clauses

This is where most firms find immediate time savings. AI writing tools can produce a first draft of a client update letter, a standard demand letter, or a routine completion confirmation in under two minutes — based on a prompt you write in plain English.

You still review it, adjust the tone, and make it accurate to the matter. But you're editing a finished draft rather than starting from a blank page. For a busy fee earner sending 20 to 30 emails a day, that's a meaningful difference.

**Tool: ChatGPT (OpenAI)**
- **Cost:** Free tier available; ChatGPT Plus costs £20/month
- **Best for:** Drafting correspondence, summarising documents you paste in, writing first drafts of standard letters
- **Honest limitation:** Has no access to your case management system or documents unless you upload them manually. Every prompt is a fresh conversation — it doesn't remember your clients or matter history.

### Summarising Long Documents Without Losing the Important Detail

Due diligence bundles, leases, expert reports, medical records in personal injury cases — these documents are long, detailed, and time-consuming to read thoroughly every time. AI document summarisation tools can read a 60-page file and produce a structured summary with key facts, dates, and flagged risks in a few minutes.

This isn't about skipping the reading. It's about getting oriented fast so your review time is focused on what matters, not on hunting through pages for the paragraph you know is there somewhere.

**Tool: Claude (Anthropic)**
- **Cost:** Free tier available; Claude Pro costs £18/month
- **Best for:** Summarising long documents, analysing uploaded PDFs, asking specific questions about a file ("what are the payment terms in this lease?")
- **Honest limitation:** Has a context window limit — extremely large document bundles may need to be split. It also won't spot what it doesn't know to look for; your professional judgement is still the essential filter.

[Claude](https://www.google.com/search?q=Claude+pricing+review)

**Tool: Luminance**
- **Cost:** Pricing on request; enterprise-focused, typically starting around £500–£800/month for smaller firms
- **Best for:** Law firms with regular high-volume document review and due diligence work who want a purpose-built, legally trained AI tool
- **Honest limitation:** The cost structure only makes sense if your volume justifies it. For a one or two-person firm handling routine matters, the investment outweighs the return.

### Keeping Clients Updated Without Writing Every Update Yourself

Client communication is one of the biggest drivers of unbillable time in any law firm. Clients want to know what's happening, keeping them informed is good practice, and in many cases it's a regulatory expectation — but it also takes time you'd rather spend on the matter itself.

AI can draft routine progress updates based on the stage a matter has reached. If you've got a standard message for "matter progressing, awaiting response from other side," you can turn it into a personalised, professional email for each client in seconds by giving the AI the name, matter type, and one or two specific details to include.

**Tool: Notion AI**
- **Cost:** Included in Notion Plus at £9.50/month per user; free trial available
- **Best for:** Teams who want to store templates, matter notes, and AI drafting in one place — useful for building a reusable library of prompts and communication templates
- **Honest limitation:** Not a case management system. It won't pull in your live matter data automatically. You're copying and pasting information in rather than it syncing with your existing software.

[Notion AI](https://www.google.com/search?q=Notion+AI+pricing+review)

### Generating First Drafts of Contracts and Precedents

Experienced solicitors will tell you that drafting from scratch is rarely what takes the time — it's adapting a precedent to fit a specific client situation while making sure nothing important gets missed or carried over from the last matter.

AI tools can generate a working draft of a standard NDA, a simple service agreement, or a basic employment contract from a detailed prompt. You define the key terms, the parties, and the specific requirements, and the AI produces a structured draft. Your job is to review it against your professional knowledge and the matter specifics — which is much faster than building from zero.

**Tool: Harvey AI**
- **Cost:** Pricing on request; plans are tailored to firm size and usage
- **Best for:** Law firms wanting AI trained specifically on legal tasks — contract drafting, legal research summaries, matter analysis
- **Honest limitation:** Premium pricing reflects its legal focus, and it's overkill for a one or two-person firm handling mostly routine matters. The return needs to show up clearly in billable time recovered before the cost makes sense.

### Handling Routine Client Enquiries Before They Reach a Fee Earner

Every law firm fields initial enquiries that follow a predictable pattern: "how much does a will cost?", "what's involved in a straightforward conveyance?", "do I have a claim?". An AI-powered chat tool on your website can handle these questions around the clock, collect the caller's details, and pass a qualified lead to the right fee earner — without anyone picking up the phone at 9pm.

**Tool: Tidio**
- **Cost:** Free plan available; paid plans start at £29/month
- **Best for:** Small firms wanting a simple, trainable chatbot on their website to handle FAQs and capture enquiry details out of hours
- **Honest limitation:** Needs careful setup to avoid straying into anything that could be construed as legal advice. Keep it strictly to process information and enquiry collection — the AI should gather details, not advise on matters.

[Visit Tidio](https://www.tidio.com)

### Transcribing and Summarising Client Meetings and Phone Calls

File notes after client calls are non-negotiable, but writing them up takes time most fee earners don't have between appointments. AI transcription tools can record a call or meeting, produce a full transcript, and generate a structured summary — including action points — in the time it takes you to make a coffee.

A litigation solicitor taking a 45-minute client call can have a clean, organised file note ready before the next appointment, rather than reconstructing the conversation from memory at 6pm.

**Tool: Otter.ai**
- **Cost:** Free plan available (limited to 300 minutes/month); Otter Pro costs £8.33/month billed annually
- **Best for:** Transcribing and summarising client calls, meetings, and internal discussions; generating action point lists from recorded conversations
- **Honest limitation:** Accuracy drops with strong accents, background noise, or when multiple people speak at once. Always review the transcript before saving it as an official file note. Also check your data handling obligations before recording any client call — consent and confidentiality requirements apply.

## How to Get Started (Assuming You've Never Used AI in Your Practice)

1. **Pick one specific task that eats your time.** Don't try to overhaul your whole workflow at once. Choose the one thing you do repeatedly that you wish took half as long — drafting client update letters is usually the best starting point.

2. **Try Claude or ChatGPT for free this week.** Go to claude.ai or chat.openai.com and paste in a real example of the task. Write a prompt like: "Draft a client update letter for a residential conveyancing matter. The buyer is [name]. We are waiting for the local authority search results. Expected completion is still on track for [date]. Professional tone, plain English."

3. **Edit the output and note what you'd change.** The first draft won't be perfect. Adjust the prompt next time based on what was missing or off. After three or four attempts, you'll have a prompt that produces a reliable first draft every time.

4. **Save your best prompts somewhere accessible.** A simple Notion page or even a Word document works fine. A library of 10 good prompts is worth more than any single AI tool subscription.

5. **Expand to one new task per month.** Document summarisation, precedent drafting, FAQ responses — one at a time. You'll know it's working when you stop noticing the time saving because the task just isn't a problem anymore.
